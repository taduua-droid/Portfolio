Recruitment and retention plan

A feasibility funnel built from clinic counts, a phone pre-screen that releases ineligible callers early, an IRB-ready flyer, and a retention plan that removes the specific burdens this protocol creates.

How many people can one site actually enroll?

The funnel starts from a clinic database query and narrows step by step to the number a site can promise the sponsor. Most of the drop comes from one criterion: at least 8 qualifying seizures in 8 weeks on stable doses of 1 to 3 seizure drugs.

The flyer, line by line

One US Letter page, ready to submit once the bracketed fields are filled. Protocol Section 7.2 requires both the sponsor and the IRB to approve any advertisement before it’s posted.

  • “Research study” above the headline, and again in the first sentenceThe reader learns it’s research before anything else.
  • “Still having seizures?”Names the condition and promises nothing.
  • “You may be able to take part if you”Eligibility is decided at screening, never by a flyer.
  • “About once a week or more”Plain wording for 8 qualifying seizures in 8 weeks. The exact rule is checked from the diary.
  • “About 10 clinic visits and 3 phone calls over 7 months”States the time commitment up front, so people who can’t manage it don’t need to call.
  • “Investigational: it is not approved by the FDA, and it may or may not help”No claim of safety or benefit.
  • Travel and payment in ordinary type, inside the boxPayment may be mentioned, but never emphasized. The line matches the approved budget and consent form, or is removed.

Pre-screening, before anyone consents

A 13-question phone script, ordered so an ineligible caller is released early, and a log that shows which step of the funnel is failing.

Question 12 never excludes anyone. It tells the site what help to offer.

Rules before anyone is logged

  • The IRB approves how people are found and contacted, including this script word for word.
  • The log uses pre-screen IDs only. Names and phone numbers stay in a separate key in the institution’s secure system.
  • Suicidality, psychiatric history, and substance use are asked at Visit 1, in person, after consent.

Weekly review of reason codes

If N3, too few seizures, tops the list, the site is looking in the wrong place, so widen the source. If D2 or D3, schedule or transport, tops it, eligible people are saying no to the burden, so strengthen the retention offers.

Keeping people in the study

People leave when the study costs their family more than it gives back. Each action below removes a specific cost this protocol creates, and anything involving money, transport, or written material gets IRB approval first.

Judgment calls

Decisions that keep the numbers honest and the participants protected.

Quote the funnel, not an impression

Sites that promise from memory tend to overpromise. The number given to the sponsor comes with the date of the database query and the assumptions that carry the most weight.

Sensitive questions wait for consent

The phone script asks only what’s needed to rule someone out. Psychiatric history and suicidality are covered at Visit 1, in person, after consent.

Count the diary at Visit 2

Checking the seizure count halfway through baseline means nobody learns at Visit 3 that they were never going to qualify.
